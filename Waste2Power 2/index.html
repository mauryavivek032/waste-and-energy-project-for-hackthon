ENVIRONMENT GUIDE · 01

💧 Clean Water

How responsible waste habits help protect rivers, lakes and groundwater.

💧
WHY IT MATTERS

Water is a shared resource

Clean water is essential for drinking, cooking, growing food and keeping ecosystems alive. Waste that is dumped on roads, in drains or near water bodies can be carried by rain into rivers and lakes. Some batteries, electronics, oils, paints and chemical containers may release harmful substances if handled badly. Plastic waste can break into smaller pieces and persist in the environment.

What you can do

  • Separate waste at source. Keep recyclables, wet waste and hazardous items in different containers.
  • Never pour oil or chemicals into drains. Follow local disposal instructions for paints, cleaners and automotive fluids.
  • Keep e-waste and batteries out of regular bins. Use an authorised collection or take-back service.
  • Reduce plastic litter. Carry a reusable bottle and bag, and put wrappers in a bin.
  • Prevent drain blockages. Do not throw food scraps, wipes or packaging into street drains.

Waste2Power action

Use Scan Waste to identify an item, then check the disposal guidance before you throw it away. Keep hazardous materials separate and contact a suitable collection service.

Small habit: carry a reusable water bottle and dispose of waste responsibly.

This page provides general awareness, not water-quality testing. Never assume water is safe to drink based on appearance alone.

ENVIRONMENT GUIDE · 02

⚡ Save Energy

Use electricity thoughtfully and understand how reuse and recycling can reduce resource demand.

⚡
WHY IT MATTERS

Energy saved is energy we do not need to generate

Producing electricity and manufacturing new products require resources. Reusing a working device or repairing an item can extend its life and reduce demand for new materials. Recycling properly sorted materials can also reduce the need to extract and process some raw materials, although the energy benefit depends on the material and recycling process. Waste-to-energy is not suitable for every waste type, and recycling should not be confused with burning waste in the open.

Everyday energy-saving habits

  • Switch off unused devices. Turn off lights, fans and chargers when they are not needed.
  • Choose efficient settings. Use power-saving mode and sensible screen brightness on phones and laptops.
  • Repair before replacing. Get a device checked by a qualified technician if repair is safe and practical.
  • Reuse or donate working items. Clear personal data first and pass devices to a trusted recipient.
  • Recycle correctly. Send end-of-life electronics to an authorised collection or recycling channel.

Waste2Power action

Log materials in Scan Waste and explore the Energy & Impact dashboard. Any energy values shown in this demo are estimates for learning—not measured electricity savings or a guaranteed power output.

Small habit: enable power-saving mode and switch off lights when leaving a room.

Actual energy savings depend on the device, material, local recycling system and process used.

ENVIRONMENT GUIDE · 03

🌿 Healthy Soil

Keep soil productive by preventing litter and managing organic waste well.

🌿
WHY IT MATTERS

Healthy soil supports plants and life

Soil helps plants grow, stores water and provides habitat for many organisms. When mixed waste is dumped on land, plastics and other persistent materials can remain for a long time. Some electronic components, batteries and chemical products may contaminate soil if they are damaged or disposed of improperly. Food and garden scraps, on the other hand, can often be composted when they are collected separately and managed correctly.

Protect the soil

  • Separate wet waste. Keep fruit peels, vegetable scraps and suitable garden waste apart from plastic and metal.
  • Compost suitable organics. Use a properly managed home or community compost system and follow local guidance.
  • Keep hazardous items separate. Batteries, electronics, oils and chemicals should not go into compost or ordinary soil.
  • Reduce litter. Use bins and keep wrappers, glass and broken items away from gardens and open land.
  • Reuse materials where practical. Repair or donate usable goods rather than discarding them too early.

Waste2Power action

Choose Organic when logging clean food scraps, and follow the composting or local collection guidance. Keep contaminated or unknown materials separate until a suitable handler advises you.

Small habit: collect fruit and vegetable scraps separately for suitable composting.

Do not compost batteries, e-waste, treated wood, chemicals, or materials contaminated with unknown substances.

ENVIRONMENT GUIDE · 04

🌬️ Fresh Air

Reduce avoidable air pollution by choosing safer waste handling and cleaner everyday habits.

🌬️
WHY IT MATTERS

Clean air helps people and ecosystems

Air pollution can come from transport, industry, dust and the burning of waste. Burning plastic, cables, batteries or electronic devices in the open can release dangerous smoke and pollutants. Smoke can affect nearby residents and workers, while particles can settle on land and water. Sorting waste and sending it to suitable reuse, recycling or treatment services helps avoid unsafe disposal. Trees and green spaces can provide shade and support local ecosystems, but they do not replace the need to prevent pollution at its source.

Actions for cleaner air

  • Never burn waste. Do not burn plastic, wires, batteries, leaves mixed with rubbish or electronics.
  • Repair and reuse. Extend product life where practical instead of replacing usable equipment.
  • Use authorised e-waste channels. Do not dismantle or heat electronic parts at home.
  • Reduce unnecessary trips. Walk, cycle or share transport for suitable short journeys when safe.
  • Keep dust and litter controlled. Store waste in suitable containers and follow local collection rules.

Waste2Power action

Use the E-Waste Guide to explore safer disposal choices and the Collection Route section to understand how grouped pickups may reduce unnecessary collection trips. The route displayed in this prototype is illustrative unless connected to verified live routing data.

Small habit: choose repair or reuse where practical, and never burn waste.

If waste is burning or producing fumes, keep away and contact the appropriate local emergency or municipal service.

🐄 Feed animals or use in the farm · जानवरों को खिलाएँ या खेत में उपयोग करें

Fresh, clean food waste that is fit to eat, such as vegetable peels, fruit scraps and leftover roti or rice, can be given to animals. Do not give spoiled, mouldy, salty, spicy or oily food, or anything with plastic. If it is not fit for animals, use it in the farm: make compost (natural manure) and mix it into the soil. It makes the soil fertile and reduces the need for chemical fertilisers.

ताज़ा और साफ़ खाने का कचरा जो खाने योग्य हो, जैसे सब्ज़ियों के छिलके, फलों के टुकड़े और बची हुई रोटी या चावल, जानवरों को दिया जा सकता है। सड़ा-गला, फफूंद लगा, ज़्यादा नमकीन, मसालेदार या तैलीय खाना, और प्लास्टिक वाला कुछ भी जानवरों को न दें। जो कचरा जानवरों को देने लायक न हो, उसे खेत में इस्तेमाल करें: कम्पोस्ट (प्राकृतिक खाद) बनाकर मिट्टी में मिलाएँ। इससे मिट्टी उपजाऊ बनती है और रासायनिक खाद की ज़रूरत कम होती है।
